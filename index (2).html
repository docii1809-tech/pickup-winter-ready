BUYING GUIDE 01

Choosing a jump starter for a truck

The right jump pack depends on the engine and battery—not just the biggest number printed on the box.

Check these before you buy

  • Engine compatibility: Confirm the manufacturer's stated gasoline or diesel engine range covers your exact engine.
  • Safety protections: Look for reverse-polarity, short-circuit, over-current, and temperature protections. Verify the exact model's documentation.
  • Cold-weather limits: Check the specified operating and charging temperature ranges. Lithium batteries may need warming before use in severe cold.
  • Cable reach and clamps: Make sure the leads can reach your battery terminals and clamps fit securely.
  • Recharge and storage: Check how often the unit needs recharging and how the manufacturer says to store it.
Don't shop by peak amps alone. Peak-amp figures are not a universal apples-to-apples performance test. Match the model to the manufacturer's engine guidance and independent test results.

BUYING GUIDE 02

Choosing a portable tire inflator

For truck tires, airflow and duty cycle often tell you more than a high maximum-PSI claim.

Compare these specifications

  • Airflow (CFM or L/min): Higher airflow can reduce inflation time, but compare ratings measured at similar pressures.
  • Duty cycle: This tells you how long the compressor can run before it needs to cool. Follow the stated rest periods.
  • Power source: Battery-powered, 12V accessory plug, and direct battery-clamp models have different use cases and power limits.
  • Hose and cord reach: Measure whether the supplied hose and power cable can reach all four tires.
  • Auto shutoff and gauge: Helpful features, but check independent gauge accuracy and don't ignore your vehicle's recommended cold tire pressure.
Truck tire reminder: Use the tire pressure specified on the vehicle placard or owner's manual—not the maximum pressure molded into the tire sidewall.

BUYING GUIDE 03

Recovery boards and winter essentials

A shovel and traction aid can be useful, but equipment does not replace safe judgment or proper recovery training.

What to look for

  • Board dimensions: Check length, width, thickness, and whether they suit your tires and storage space.
  • Material and rated load: Read manufacturer instructions and limitations. Avoid relying on unsupported weight claims.
  • Surface design: Consider tread engagement, grip, handles, and whether the board can be cleaned and stored easily.
  • Recovery basics: A compact shovel, gloves, flashlight, reflective warning device, and suitable rated recovery equipment can round out a kit.
  • Safe use: Never stand in the path of a tensioned recovery strap or use an un-rated attachment point. Follow the vehicle and recovery-equipment manufacturers' instructions.
Safety: Never use a kinetic recovery rope or strap unless you understand its use, rated attachment points, exclusion zones, and the manufacturer's procedure. When in doubt, call a qualified recovery professional.
